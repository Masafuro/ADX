In our last log (*"From PA0 to ADX\_P01"*), we locked down **ADX-BUS-SPEC-v4.2**—the logical pin-naming layer that lets sketches and expansion Cards move across 8-bit AVR, 32-bit RISC-V, and RP2040 cores without dragging silicon-specific port names onto silkscreens.

Now the physical half of the ADX standard is catching up.

Today we published **8858 Form Factor (v0.1-alpha)** and verified the manufacturing Gerbers for **ADX Reference CARD v0**—the official physical blueprint that prints every keep-out, coordinate, bus pinout, and optical alignment marker directly onto FR4. Rather than picking an arbitrary PCB rectangle, the final standard emerged from resolving **four sharp engineering contradictions** pulling in opposite directions.

---

## 1. Contradiction #1: Panelization vs. Compute Modules vs. Cable Escape

When you try to unify low-cost microcontrollers (**ATtiny1616**, **ESP32**, **RP2040**), industrial **PRO** isolated cards, and compact Linux compute modules (**Raspberry Pi CM0**, **Pi Zero**, and **CM4 / CM5**) under a single modular card footprint, every millimeter starts a fight:

- **Shrink the depth (48–50 mm)**: You can squeeze 8 boards (2×4) onto a 200 mm PCBA panel, but once you place a vertical 20-pin IDC box header on the top edge and industrial spring-clamp terminals on the bottom edge, there is barely room left for isolated DC-DC circuits or a **CM0** module.
- **Stretch the depth (60–66 mm)**: Component layout gets luxurious, but the card falls out of standard **W=70 mm** off-the-shelf enclosures—or leaves zero room behind the board for the 20-pin ribbon cable to bend and escape.

Keeping our entire X-axis intact (**88.0 mm** width, **66.0 mm** centered front I/O edge, **7.0 mm** top-edge offset to the 20-pin IDC center), we ran a full trade-off matrix across depths from 48 mm to 66 mm. One coordinate converged across every constraint at once: **88.0 × 58.0 mm (the 8858 Form Factor)**.

| Parameter | 8858 Specification (v0.1-alpha) | Why It Locks In |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Board Outline** | **88.0 × 58.0 mm** (3465 × 2283 mil, C0 corners) | Aspect ratio **1.52 : 1**—matching bridge-size playing cards (89 × 58 mm) and Raspberry Pi Model B (85 × 56 mm). |
| **200 mm PCBA Panel** | **2 × 3 = 6 pcs/panel**, 4-side 5 mm rails → **186 × 184 mm** | Near-square (**1.01 : 1**) panel maximizes reflow rigidity and stencil printing stability for straight V-cuts. |
| **Mounting Holes** | 4× M3 NPTH (Φ3.30 mm), **76.0 × 46.0 mm** pitch | 6.0 mm corner margin, Φ10.0 mm washer keep-out; metric/mil Y-pitch error drops to **-0.6 µm** (all dimensions within **12 µm**). |
| **20P IDC Center** | **(44.0, 51.0) mm** (7.0 mm from top edge) | Compatible with both SMD and THT vertical box headers; leaves **46.5 mm** clear below the shroud body. |
| **Module Fit** | MCU / PRO Cards / Pi Zero / **CM0** / rear **CM4–CM5** | **CM0** leaves 11.5 mm below IDC for an inline front USB-C; **Pi Zero** (30 mm) clears M3 keep-outs (36 mm span); **CM4/CM5** mount on the rear face in vertical PLC racks for natural chimney cooling. |
| **Enclosure Target** | **W=70 mm** commercial cases & **100 × 70 mm** PLC racks | Fits **Takachi MB7-5-10** with ~6.2 mm rear IDC cable escape and 2-board stacking, plus **YM10-3-7** and **SW-125**. |

Accepting one explicit trade-off made everything else click: we dropped tight W=65 mm plastic boxes like the **Takachi SW-100** (whose draft-angle inner width narrows to **57.8 mm**—too tight for a 58 mm board and zero room for rear ribbon cables anyway) and committed cleanly to **W=70 mm** enclosures and **100 mm tall × 70 mm deep** vertical slide-in racks.

## 2. Contradiction #2: Automated Post-PCBA Vision Without Copper Fiducials

Every board in an open hardware ecosystem still has to be tested after assembly. Our goal is low-cost, open-source post-PCBA inspection—LED checks, visual sanity checks, and bed-of-nails probing—using a commodity **720p/1080p USB webcam**, **OpenCV**, and **3D-printed test jigs**.

That immediately runs into four physical traps with conventional PCB fiducials:

1. **Panel-rail fiducials vanish** the moment boards are depaneled along the V-cut.
2. **Exposed bare-copper pads oxidize** on low-cost **OSP** (Organic Solderability Preservative) finishes, wrecking vision contrast over time.
3. **Copper under soldermask fails across colors**—usable under light green resist, nearly invisible under matte black or white resist.
4. **Corner M3 mounting holes get occluded** by clamp fingers and jig guide pins.

The resolution was separating the problem cleanly: **these markers are not for high-speed SMT pick-and-place machines—they are for post-PCBA testing.** On a 3D-printed test jig (±0.5–1.0 mm mechanical play), silkscreen registration accuracy (±0.15–0.25 mm) is more than tight enough, and matte white silkscreen gives diffuse, glare-resistant contrast regardless of whether the board uses ENIG, HASL, or OSP.

So **8858** embeds a two-layer optical specification directly into the four corners of the left/right underside SMD-Only zones:

- **Layer 1 — Optional Marker Zone (MAY)**: Four **6.0 × 6.0 mm** reserved boxes at **M1 (6, 44)**, **M2 (82, 44)**, **M3 (6, 14)**, and **M4 (82, 14) mm**. Sharing the exact X coordinates (**6.0 / 82.0 mm**) of the M3 mounting holes keeps the layout grid clean, maintains **1.00 mm** clearance to both the 3.0 mm card guides and Φ10 mm M3 keep-outs, and puts a **3.0 mm** buffer between the markers and the central 66 mm component area so tall electrolytic caps or terminal blocks don't cast shadows across them.
- **Layer 2 — Official Optical Profile (SHOULD)**: Three **Φ4.0 mm concentric bullseyes** (Φ1.20 mm center dot, 0.90 mm dark ring, Φ4.00 / Φ3.00 mm outer ring) at **M1–M3**, paired with a **4.0 × 4.0 mm DataMatrix ECC200 (10×10)** at **M4**.

## 3. Contradiction #3: Side Detection, Rotation, and 4 mm Board IDs

Two final vision headaches show up the moment you inspect double-sided boards on a bench:

- **Symmetry breaks orientation**: Two markers cannot distinguish 0° from 180°, and symmetric four-corner markers cannot tell whether a board is face-up or face-down. Asking PCB designers to manually mirror coordinates on the bottom layer is a recipe for CAD mistakes.
- **Full part-number strings break tiny barcodes**: Cramming a string like **ADX-CARD-REF-V0** into a 4.0 × 4.0 mm QR code shrinks the modules below reliable silkscreen resolution on budget fabs.

![ADX 8858 Chiral Optical Marker Geometry](2026-11-23-chiral-markers.png "Fig 1: Reusing identical CAD coordinates on Top and Bottom silkscreen reverses the 76.0 × 30.0 mm scalene right triangle from CW (Top) to CCW (Bottom) when physically flipped.")

Putting the three bullseyes at **M1–M3** forms a **76.0 × 30.0 mm scalene right triangle**—and unlocks a clean geometric trick:

1. **Chiral (handedness) side auto-detection**: Designers place bottom-side markers on **BottomSilkscreen** at the **exact same CAD coordinates** (M1–M3 bullseyes, M4 DataMatrix) with **no manual mirroring**. When you physically flip the PCB in front of a camera, **M4** jumps to the bottom-left and the triangle's winding order (**M1 → M2 long leg → M3 short leg**) reverses from **Clockwise (CW)** on Top to **Counter-Clockwise (CCW)** on Bottom. Because chirality is invariant under all 2D rotations (0°–360°), OpenCV can uniquely resolve **board side, rotation angle, and affine transform from the three bullseyes alone**—before even running a barcode decoder.
2. **6-digit numeric compression for 0.40 mm cells**: Instead of stuffing ASCII model names into **M4**, we use **ISO/IEC 16022 ECC200 (10×10)** numeric-pair encoding. Three data codewords pack up to **6 decimal digits** (1,000,000 unique Board IDs; on Reference CARD v0, Top is **100010** and Bottom is **100011**) alongside five Reed-Solomon error-correction codewords, keeping every module a chunky **0.40 × 0.40 mm** (with the solid L-finder locked to Left and Bottom edges). Mapping an integer ID to a board name and test recipe stays cleanly on the PC side.

> **Key Takeaway:**
> When you design alignment markers specifically for post-PCBA testing rather than SMT pick-and-place, switching from exposed copper to silkscreen removes finish and resist-color fragility—and an asymmetric 3-bullseye triangle lets you detect Top vs. Bottom automatically without manual CAD mirroring.

## 4. Contradiction #4: Clean EDA Import Without Fragile Barcode Toolchains

Generic online barcode generators output PNGs with arbitrary quiet zones or SVGs built from dozens of touching micro-rectangles that render seams or scale incorrectly when imported into **EasyEDA Pro** or **KiCad**. They also rarely handle inverted white-on-dark silkscreen polarity or ink-bleed compensation cleanly.

To keep the standard self-contained, we wrote a zero-dependency pure-Python CLI tool—**software/tools/gen\_adx\_datamatrix.py**—that implements the **10×10 ECC200** encoder, **GF(256)** Reed-Solomon generator, and a built-in round-trip syndrome decoder self-check from scratch. It traces contiguous ink regions into **seam-free merged orthogonal SVG polygons** at exact physical millimeters, with **0 mm file-internal quiet zone** so dropping it onto a silkscreen layer at **4.0 × 4.0 mm** never mis-scales (and an optional **--bleed-comp-mm** flag shrinks exposed ink edges to compensate for fab silkscreen spread):

```bash
# Generate exact 4.0 x 4.0 mm merged-contour SVG + transparent PNG for Board ID 100010
python3 software/tools/gen_adx_datamatrix.py 100010 -o hardware/CARD/Reference/data --preview

# Optional: apply 0.03 mm per-edge silkscreen bleed compensation
python3 software/tools/gen_adx_datamatrix.py 100010 --bleed-comp-mm 0.03 -o out/
```

Under the hood, every generated symbol is decoded back through the Reed-Solomon syndrome verifier before a single file is written:

```python
def encode_datamatrix_10x10(payload: str):
    data_cws = encode_ascii_codewords_10x10(payload)   # 3 codewords (up to 6 digits)
    ecc_cws  = compute_rs_ecc5(data_cws)               # 5 GF(256) Reed-Solomon codewords
    all_cws  = data_cws + ecc_cws

    inner = [[0] * 8 for _ in range(8)]
    for cw, coords in zip(all_cws, _UTAH_8X8):
        for bit_idx, (r, c) in enumerate(coords):
            inner[r][c] = (cw >> (7 - bit_idx)) & 1

    # Assemble 10x10 with L-shaped solid finder (left & bottom) + clock tracks
    grid = [[0] * 10 for _ in range(10)]
    for c in range(10):
        grid[0][c] = 1 if (c % 2 == 0) else 0
        grid[9][c] = 1
    for r in range(10):
        grid[r][0] = 1
        grid[r][9] = 1 if (r % 2 == 1) else 0
    for r in range(8):
        for c in range(8):
            grid[r + 1][c + 1] = inner[r][c]
    return grid, all_cws
```

Because the default **silk** output maps black pixels directly to silkscreen ink ON (and transparent to bare resist OFF), importing the exact same asset gives Light-on-Dark contrast on green/black/blue PCBs and Dark-on-Light contrast on white PCBs automatically.

## 5. Gerber-Verified Reference Hardware & Next Steps

The full **8858 Form Factor** specification and **ADX Reference CARD v0** manufacturing package (**Gerber\_PCB1\_2026-11-23.zip**, exported from EasyEDA Pro v4.1.105) have been verified across every Gerber and Excellon drill layer:

- **Mechanical & Drill**: Exact **88.000 × 58.000 mm** outline, M3 pitch **76.000 × 46.000 mm**, 20-pin IDC center at **(44.000, 51.000) mm** (-0.4 µm delta).
- **Copper & Keep-outs**: Unbroken top/bottom GND pours with **0.612 mm** long-edge margin, **3.104 mm** card-guide clearance, **R = 5.10 mm** M3 copper pull-back, and uniform copper under all four optical marker zones.
- **Silkscreen & Vision Check**: Zero silkscreen-over-pad collisions on either side; both **100010** (Top, CW) and **100011** (Bottom, CCW) DataMatrix polygons pass Reed-Solomon ECC verification with over **1.9 mm** of clear quiet zone.

Next up is turning these Gerbers into physical boards across multiple soldermask colors and OSP finishes—then putting a cheap USB webcam and **OpenCV / zxing-cpp** to work benchmarking real-world decode rates, lighting angles, and silkscreen ink bleed at **0.40 mm/cell**.
