In yesterday's log (*"The Art of Subtraction"*), we stripped our USB Core board (**`ADX CORE-U rev4`**) down to dual Type-C ports, an eFuse, a `CH342K`, four LEDs, and **one** 20-pin IDC expansion bus (`CN1`).

That left us with an obvious practical trade-off: with zero onboard screw terminals or loose pin headers, you cannot wire up even a single sensor or MOSFET until you plug an expansion Card into `CN1`.

Today, we finished and generated Gerbers for that very first companion board—**`ADX Prototype CARD v0`**—and finalized **`ADX-BUS-SPEC-v4.2`**, the logical pin-naming layer printed across its silkscreen.

---

## 1. The Challenge: Three Friction Points Before Soldering Pin 1

Designing a "simple" prototyping board quickly ran into three non-obvious problems:

1. **MCU port names don't survive across architectures**: Calling a bus line `PA0`, `PB2`, or `PC19` makes sense on a single-chip project. Once your Core lineup spans an 8-bit `ATtiny1616`, a 32-bit RISC-V `CH32X035`, and a planned `RP2040`, printing native silicon port names on expansion Cards breaks portability immediately.
2. **8854's underside keep-out leaves awkward margins**: On the **8854 Form Factor** (88.0 mm × 54.0 mm), the underside outside the central 66.0 mm I/O edge is an **SMD-Only zone** (no protruding through-hole leads) so enclosure guide rails and snap-fit clips can slide under cleanly. On a conventional perfboard, those left and right wings just become wasted fiberglass.
3. **One more millimeter on the IDC header**: Moving `CORE-U rev4` to an all-SMD vertical 20-pin IDC box header revealed that our original `Y = 48.0 mm` center coordinate (6.0 mm from the top edge) was tight for SMD outer solder fillets and V-cut edge clearance.

---

## 2. `ADX-BUS-SPEC-v4.2`: Why `S00`, `P01`–`P10`, and the `#define P00` Trap

Version `v4.2` maintains **full electrical and physical backward compatibility** with `v4.1`, while inserting an MCU-independent **Logical Pin ID** layer between physical IDC pin numbers (`Pin 1`–`20`) and guaranteed peripheral functions:

| IDC Pin | Logical ID | Guaranteed Bus Function | Notes / Shielding Role |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | `GND` | 0V Return | Outer edge ESD / mechanical shield |
| **2** | **`S00`** | `OWD` (`UPDI` / `SWDIO`) | **System debug only — Cards MUST leave N.C.** |
| **3** | `P01` | `AIN` / 3.3V GPIO | Coplanar shielded analog input (flanked by Pins 1 & 4 `GND`) |
| **4** | `GND` | 0V Return | Analog / clock isolation guard |
| **5** | `P02` | `EXTCLK` / `SWCLK` / GPIO | Clock waveguide (flanked by Pins 4 & 6 `GND`; 0 Ω DNP recommended on Cards) |
| **6** | `GND` | 0V Return | Clock / comm isolation guard |
| **7–8** | `P03`–`P04` | `UART_TX` / `UART_RX` | Async serial pair (or 3.3V GPIO) |
| **9–10** | `P05`–`P06` | `I2C_SCL` / `I2C_SDA` | 3.3V open-drain I2C (pull-ups required) |
| **11** | `GND` | 0V Return | Open-drain (I2C) to push-pull (SPI) barrier |
| **12–15** | `P07`–`P10` | `SPI_CS` / `MOSI` / `MISO` / `SCK` | High-speed SPI bus group (or 3.3V GPIO) |
| **16** | `GND` | 0V Return | Short-circuit barrier wall guarding logic lines from `VBUS` |
| **17–20** | `VBUS` | 5.0V Power (2–3A) | 2×2 solid copper grid (no 3.3V rail on bus — PoL only) |

Three small design decisions did most of the heavy lifting here:

- **Flat `P01`–`P10` instead of `D`/`A` splits**: Legacy Arduino numbering splits pins into digital (`D`) and analog (`A`), which gets confusing fast when `A0` is digital and I2C shares analog pins. A neutral flat series (`P01`–`P10`, uniform 3-character alignment) keeps silkscreens and schematics clean, while clearly separating the **guaranteed bus contract** (works on every Core) from MCU-specific bonus features (extra ADC/PWM/DAC channels).
- **`S00` visually quarantines the programming line**: Power rails stay unnumbered, and Pin 2 gets its own prefix (`S` = System). Even at a glance on a cramped silkscreen, `S00` does not look like a user GPIO.
- **Why firmware macros MUST be `ADX_P01`, never bare `P01`**: We originally considered defining bare `P01`–`P10` constants in board headers. Then we checked common Arduino I2C GPIO expander libraries—`PCF8575` and `PCA9555` libraries routinely ship global `#define P00 0` through `#define P07 7` and `#define P10 8`. Because C preprocessor macros ignore C++ `namespace` boundaries, bare `Pxx` identifiers are a ticking compile-time landmine. Prefixing every pin with `ADX_` (and intentionally leaving `S00` undefined in user headers) avoids the collision cleanly:

```cpp
// Swap one header line when moving a sketch between Core architectures
#include "ADX_CORE_U.h"     // or "ADX_CORE_CX5.h"

void setup() {
  pinMode(ADX_P07, OUTPUT);       // Logical ID (or alias: ADX_CS)
}

void loop() {
  int raw = analogRead(ADX_P01);  // Guaranteed shielded ADC (or alias: ADX_AIN)
  digitalWrite(ADX_P07, raw > 512);
}
```

## 3. Turning a Keep-Out Into a Feature: Inside `ADX Prototype CARD v0`

Once `v4.2` settled, we laid out `ADX Prototype CARD v0` as the first board carrying the new labels—and looked for a way to make every square millimeter of 8854 useful without violating a single enclosure keep-out:

### 1. Dual-Pitch Universal Deck (342 THT + 240 SMD)
- **Center `66.0 mm` zone**: Standard **2.54 mm pitch THT** grid (`12×26 + (5×3)×2` = **342 independent plated holes**, `Φ0.91 mm` drill / `Φ1.52 mm` pad) safely inside the through-hole permitted span.
- **Left & right underside SMD-Only wings**: Instead of leaving those side zones blank, we populated the **Top Layer only** with **1.27 mm (50 mil) pitch SMD** arrays (`(24×5)×2` = **240 independent `0.89 × 0.89 mm` pads**, `~0.38 mm` gap). That gives you dedicated landings for SOIC breakouts, SOT-23 transistors, or 0603/0805 passives while keeping the underside flat for enclosure rails—with `3.10 mm` edge clearance for 3.0 mm card guides and `5.53 mm` clearance to the nearest M3 mounting center.

### 2. 2×20P Unrolled Breakout (`H1`) & Unbroken Bottom GND
- Directly beneath the 20-pin IDC header (`U1`), a `2×20P` breakout row unrolls all 20 pins in flat-ribbon wire order (`Pin 1` to `Pin 20`), giving **two vertically bridged holes per signal**—one to solder circuit wires, one to clip an oscilloscope probe or jumper.
- Every signal trace between `U1` and `H1` stays strictly on the **Top Layer**, leaving an **unbroken Bottom-Layer GND plane** underneath. The four `VBUS` pins flow through a solid top-layer copper pour straight into all 8 `5VBUS` breakout holes.

### 3. Silkscreen as Poka-Yoke (Visual Error-Proofing)
- **`S00` carries a double underline** as a visual stop sign (*do not wire tactile switches or pull-downs here*).
- **`GPIO3V3` sits directly above the boxed `5VBUS`** to reinforce the Point-of-Load (PoL) rule: the bus brings 5V power only, while all I/O runs at 3.3V LVCMOS—regulate 3.3V locally on-card.
- **`ADX_*`** in the top-left corner cues the firmware macro prefix, and the bottom silkscreen prints exact pitches and matrix counts (`2.54mm: 12x26+(5x3)x2`, `1.27mm: 24x5x2`) so you never have to count holes by hand.

> **Key Takeaway:**
> Mechanical keep-outs don't have to mean wasted PCB real estate, and silkscreen doesn't have to be decorative. Turning an underside THT exclusion zone into top-side 1.27 mm SMD pads—and printing voltage boundaries (`GPIO3V3` vs. `5VBUS`) plus `S00` warnings right next to the pads—prevents real bench mistakes before power is ever applied.

## 4. One More Millimeter & the `$0.97` Bare-Board Economics

Two practical notes from finalizing the Gerbers today:

1. **8854 IDC Center Moved `1.0 mm` Inward (`Y: 48.0 → 47.0 mm`)**:
   In our November 9 *Reference CARD v0* prototype, the 20-pin IDC center sat 6.0 mm from the top board edge (`Y = 48.0 mm`). Once `CORE-U rev4` adopted an **SMD vertical 20P box header**, 6.0 mm left uncomfortably little clearance for outer SMD fillets and V-cut scoring. Shifting the 8854 standard center down by **1.0 mm** to **`(44.0, 47.0) mm`** (7.0 mm from the top edge; `[1732, 1850] mil`, a `-10 µm` metric/imperial delta) solves it for both SMD and THT headers. `Prototype CARD v0` is the first Gerber package built to `(44.0, 47.0) mm`.
2. **Low-Cost ENIG Fabrication vs. Low-Ticket Reality**:
   Using 8854's standard `2×3` V-cut panelization (`6 pcs/panel` × 20 panels = **120 bare boards**) with an **ENIG** finish (far nicer than HASL for hand-soldering 1.27 mm SMD pads and shelf storage), JLCPCB came out to **`$116.55` shipped—`$0.97` per board**.
   The honest catch: on a sub-dollar bare PCB, a naive 4× retail multiplier (`$3.88`) gets eaten alive by fixed per-order costs like ESD bags, pick-and-pack time, and payment fees. Making cheap open-hardware prototyping accessories sustainable to distribute is a separate puzzle from making them cheap to fab.
